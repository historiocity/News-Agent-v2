Polish prosecutors open a terrorism investigation as national mourning begins

International / NATO ally Follow-up Independent corroboration

Independent accounts compared: 5. Continuation. Trigger: national prosecutor opens a terrorism investigation; police identify the three bodies at the suspect’s home; charging posture and medical status clarified overnight; mourning begins.

Poland began two national days of mourning on Sunday after a gunman killed six people on Kraków’s Main Market Square. The national prosecutor’s office said it had opened an investigation into the killing of six people with a firearm and the attempted murder of many others, including police officers, “this act constituting a terrorist offence,” The Guardian reported. Prosecutor Tomasz Waszczuk told ABC News that prosecutors are preparing six murder charges over the square deaths, and that the count could rise to nine if investigators establish the same attacker also killed his parents and sister. Asked whether anyone else was involved, he said the evidence so far does not point to other people. Justice Minister Waldemar Żurek, quoted by NBC News, said the suspect “acted with the aim of seriously intimidating a large number of people, making this a terrorist offence,” and faces a separate murder investigation over three relatives found dead at his home.

Police confirmed Sunday that those three were his father, 53, mother, 52, and sister, 21, in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, about 100 miles northeast of Kraków. Counterterrorism officers spent the night searching the home for explosives and finished Sunday morning, ABC reported; the Ostrowiec prosecutor’s office, cited by The Independent, said a significant amount of ammunition and seven firearms were seized. The 24-year-old Polish suspect remains in critical condition under guard after several operations and has not yet been formally detained because of his medical condition. Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński again said he was fascinated by far-right, “ultra-nationalistic” views. Defense Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz publicly denied circulating claims that the attacker was a Polish soldier.

Four women and two men were killed in the square, including a 32-year-old British man. Names of the other dead have not been fully released; one dead woman remains unidentified. Desks still disagree on the wounded total (see box). Two officers who confronted the gunman are hospitalized; Prime Minister Donald Tusk named them as senior constables Jakub Kulik and Artur Morański. Police told ABC that one officer was brought out of a medically induced coma Sunday morning and the other remained in one, and appealed for AB Rh+ blood for Kulik. The square was still partly cordoned off Sunday; European Commission flags in Brussels were lowered to half-staff. The U.S. State Department has said it was not aware of any American citizens harmed; U.S. Ambassador Tom Rose posted condolences.

US channel: Poland is a frontline NATO ally. No U.S. nationals have been named among the dead or wounded.

What to watch: the charging document that specifies the terrorism counts; whether the family murders are joined; when doctors clear the suspect for questioning; the two officers’ medical status.

Divergence — casualty totals and the terrorism label

What is in dispute. Facts dispute on the wounded: Sunday copies do not agree, with AP’s Sunday packages and early Deutsche Welle at 15, NBC at 16, The Guardian at 17, and ABC reporting 11 Polish wounded (two of them later discharged) plus five foreign nationals from Britain, Austria, Germany, Portugal and Russia among the injured. The six square deaths are not contested; one dead woman remains unidentified, so the full nationality mix of the dead is not settled. Significance dispute: Polish officials (Tusk, the defense minister, the national prosecutor, Żurek) call it a terrorist offence; the established facts are still a lone gunman with far-right views and family members dead at his home, and Waszczuk says evidence so far does not show others involved.

Why the vantage differs. Prosecutors need a legal frame for a terrorism investigation and any follow-on detentions. Wire and Sunday newspaper desks are updating casualty tallies from police briefings that themselves moved overnight; ABC had later hospital and blood-drive detail than the pre-dawn wraps. Timing: European desks and U.S. Sunday rewrites had later governor, justice-minister, and prosecutor comments than the overnight AP summary.

What it means for the reader. Treat the six square deaths, the family identification, the overnight home search, the seven-firearm seizure, and the opening of a terrorism investigation as confirmed. Treat the wounded count and the full nationality mix as not yet settled, and the terrorism label as the prosecutors’ legal theory rather than a proven conspiracy. The observation that settles both disputes is the charging document and a single official casualty table.

Saudi defenses intercept missiles; shrapnel damages an Aramco tower in Riyadh

International / energy New subject Independent corroboration

Independent accounts compared: 3.

Saudi Arabia’s Energy Ministry said Sunday that falling shrapnel damaged an Aramco administrative building in Riyadh after an “attempted” attack, with no casualties and limited damage, Al Jazeera, CNBC, and AP reported. A Reuters witness, cited by CNBC, saw blown-out windows across several floors of the Aramco Tower in the King Abdullah Financial District; AFP witnesses, carried into Sunday copy, described shattered glass and facade damage across multiple floors. Multiple loud booms were heard in the capital after Saudi Civil Defense issued, then lifted, early-warning alerts; similar alerts went out and were later lifted in the oil-rich Eastern Province, including Dhahran, home to Aramco’s headquarters.

The Saudi-led coalition fighting Yemen’s Iran-aligned Houthis said its air defenses had destroyed missiles launched toward the kingdom. Al Jazeera, citing coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki’s posts, said two missiles were launched toward Riyadh and another toward the Eastern Province, and that the coalition also intercepted two missiles in the south and a drone. AP described intercepts of two ballistic missiles and two cruise missiles targeting Riyadh and other regions. There was no immediate public claim of responsibility from the Houthis, and Aramco itself had not commented at the time those reports were published.

The incident sits inside a wider war that restarted after a 2022 truce collapsed in July and after U.S. and Israeli strikes on Iran earlier this year. A Houthi strike on Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport on Oct. 10 killed 13 people and wounded more than 300, both outlets note. The Houthi-run health ministry said Saturday that Saudi-led airstrikes had killed 33 people, including eight women and 13 children, in a village east of Sanaa; Al Jazeera said it could not independently verify those figures, and there was no immediate comment from the coalition.

US channel: The Houthis are Iran-aligned; damage to an Aramco building in the capital, plus alerts over the Eastern Province, is a direct oil-market and Gulf-security channel for the United States.

Divergence — how many missiles, of what kind

What is in dispute. Facts dispute: Al Jazeera’s account of al-Maliki’s posts describes several ballistic missiles (two toward Riyadh, one toward the Eastern Province, two in the south) plus a drone; CNBC describes two cruise missiles intercepted in Riyadh and the south; AP describes two ballistic and two cruise missiles. The Energy Ministry’s “limited” shrapnel damage and the Reuters/AFP witnesses’ blown-out windows are compatible, not contradictory. Neither outlet reported casualties or a confirmed hit on production facilities.

Why the vantage differs. The coalition is speaking through short posts that desks summarize at different times; CNBC and AFP had eyewitnesses at the tower and leaned on that color. The Houthis had not issued their own claim, so there is no attacker narrative to compare. Timing: Sunday morning, with alerts still being lifted as copy moved.

What it means for the reader. Believe that Saudi defenses engaged incoming fire and that an Aramco administrative tower in Riyadh took shrapnel damage with no reported injuries. Do not yet treat a production outage, a precise missile count, or a Houthi claim as established. The observation that settles the munitions dispute is a formal coalition statement or independently identified wreckage.

Zaporizhzhia loses its last external power line and drops onto diesel generators

International / US channel New subject Underreported Independent corroboration

Independent accounts compared: 4. Negative-check / underreported lead.

Europe’s largest nuclear plant lost its last remaining external electricity supply early Sunday and is running on emergency diesel generators to keep nuclear fuel cool. Ukrainian state operator Energoatom said the 330 kV Ferrosplavna-1 line disconnected at 2:36 a.m. local time and that an automatic reconnect failed, Ukrainska Pravda and Ukrinform reported. Energoatom counts this as the 16th complete external-power blackout at Zaporizhzhia in 2026. The International Atomic Energy Agency, in a statement relayed by Anadolu Agency and carried into Arab News via Reuters, called it the 27th such loss since the war began — a different counting window, not a contradiction — and said the cause and location of the disconnection were not yet known. Director General Rafael Grossi warned of a “precarious nuclear safety situation” and said fuel reserves could run a limited number of emergency generators for about two weeks. He urged restoration of the grid connection and larger diesel reserves.

The plant produces no electricity but still needs off-site power to cool fuel; the main Dniprovska line has been unavailable since at least Oct. 23. The Russian-installed plant management said the Ferosplavna line had been shut down by “automatic protection” after damage and that radiation levels on site and in the surveillance zone were normal, a claim carried by TASS and Arab News. All six reactors remain in cold shutdown. The blackout lands three days before U.S.–Ukraine–Russia talks in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday and Wednesday, the first such meeting since February. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov has said Moscow’s delegation will include Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov plus other senior diplomats and military officials.

US channel: Nuclear safety at a Russian-occupied plant, and the composition of the Russian delegation to talks the United States is joining this week.

Divergence — why the line went down

What is in dispute. Facts dispute on the cause: the IAEA says the cause and location are unknown; Energoatom frames repeated blackouts as a consequence of the Russian occupation and of damage to connecting lines; the Russian-installed management, via TASS, now says automatic protection shut the line after damage and that radiation readings are normal. The fact of the disconnection itself, and that diesel generators are now carrying the load, is not contested.

Why the vantage differs. Energoatom is the Ukrainian state operator speaking from outside an occupied plant. The IAEA has a team on site but will not assign blame before it knows the fault location. Russian management has an interest in presenting a controlled outage even while acknowledging damage. Timing: the line dropped before dawn Sunday; early copy is still thin on engineering detail.

What it means for the reader. Believe that Zaporizhzhia is again on backup generators with roughly two weeks of fuel for a limited set of units. Do not yet treat a deliberate strike, a routine trip, or an imminent radiation accident as established. The observation that settles the cause is an IAEA update that locates the fault — or restoration of Ferrosplavna-1.
