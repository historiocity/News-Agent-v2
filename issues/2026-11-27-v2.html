Trump calls pre-election growth figures “FAKE” and demands an investigation after Commerce raises third-quarter GDP to 4.3% from 3.4%

Broader domestic institutions Core — Macro & markets New Independent corroboration

Independent accounts compared: 2 newsrooms (the AP, as carried by PBS NewsHour; CBS News), plus the Bureau of Economic Analysis release itself and X’s summary of reaction.

On Wednesday the Bureau of Economic Analysis published its second estimate of third-quarter growth: real GDP rose at a 4.3% annual rate, revised up 0.9 percentage point from the first estimate, mainly because investment and consumer spending were revised up (BEA). Second-quarter growth was 2.2%. Inflation in the release did not change: the PCE price index rose 2.1% and the core index 2.5%, both as first estimated. The next estimate is due Dec. 23.

On Thanksgiving morning Trump posted that “the REAL Numbers on the Economy, published before the Election, were FAKE,” called the change a “massive, and purposeful, error” and asked for “an immediate investigation” (AP via PBS NewsHour). His complaint is that the stronger number arrived after the Nov. 3 midterms, which gave Democrats both houses. CBS notes that he gave no evidence and that the department revises GDP and jobs data routinely (CBS News). The Commerce Department, which runs the bureau and is led by his own appointee, did not respond on the holiday.

Why it matters to you. GDP, jobs and inflation figures set what the Fed does with rates and what your bank and your investments price in. The AP says this is the latest of several attacks on data that did not match Trump’s account of the economy. An investigation of the statistical agencies, whoever runs it, would put pressure on the numbers that markets and businesses rely on.

Divergence

Dispute type: motive and process. Was this a deliberate pre-election understatement, or a normal scheduled revision?

Each side’s vantage: Trump is the party hurt by the midterm result and offers no evidence. His interest is a story that the economy was stronger than voters were told. The AP and CBS, which reported on Thursday, point to BEA’s published method: revisions come on a pre-set calendar as fuller data arrive. BEA’s own release attributes the change to upward revisions in investment and consumer spending. It does not address Trump. On X, supporters cheered the post and critics mocked it. That is reaction, not evidence.

What it means for you: nothing we read suggests the first estimate was manipulated, and Trump offered no evidence. The thing to watch is any formal step. A named investigator, or a change at the top of BEA, would be a real institutional event.

The trade office proposes Section 301 tariffs on 14 economies, from China and the EU to Japan and Vietnam, to replace the levies the Supreme Court struck down; reports differ on the China rate

World economy Core — US politics & policy New Independent corroboration

Independent accounts compared: 4 newsrooms (ABC News, CBS News, BBC, The Daily Wire), plus X’s summary of market reaction. We could not read the trade office’s notice. It is not yet in the Federal Register, and the trade office’s press page returned an error.

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer’s office announced on Wednesday that it had found the manufacturing policies of 14 economies “unreasonable” under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974: China, the European Union, Japan, India, South Korea, Taiwan, Vietnam, Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Switzerland, Bangladesh and Cambodia (ABC News). Investigations of Mexico and Norway are still open. The legal basis is new because the Supreme Court ruled in February that the emergency-powers law does not allow tariffs. The proposed duties are aimed at specific products, and a senior official said they would not stack on existing duties, except for China’s (CBS News).

The BBC’s economics editor says the rates are “upper ceilings” that are open to negotiation and are not due before January or February, after consultation. He says China’s 20% matches the rate in last year’s Trump–Xi truce, which expires in mid-January, and that Beijing has not retaliated (BBC). Capital Economics told CBS the move is “not as alarming as it looks” because it largely replaces earlier tariffs. The Daily Wire’s account leads with the countries hit and frames it as Trump’s new attempt after the Supreme Court setback (Daily Wire).

Why it matters to you. The average statutory U.S. tariff rate is now 11.2%, by the Yale Budget Lab’s count cited by CBS, and the final rates will feed into the prices of goods imported from these 14 economies next year. The incoming Speaker, Hakeem Jeffries, has said the new House will try to repeal the Trump tariffs in its first 100 days (BBC), so the final rates will be set while Democrats control Congress.

Divergence

Dispute type: factual. What is the China rate, and who is on the list?

Each source’s vantage: ABC, which spoke with a senior official, says China faces an extra 20%, which could double to 40% if Beijing breaks the truce, on top of existing 25% tech-transfer tariffs. CBS puts China’s total rate at 45%. CBS also reports that Brazil will be among the hardest hit, through duties on sugar and a new 10% levy on aircraft. ABC’s list of 14 does not include Brazil, and neither does the BBC. A summary on X said rates run “up to 40% on China, 35% on Vietnam”; we found no newsroom that gives the Vietnam figure.

What it means for you: the final notice will settle the numbers. Until then, treat 45% as one outlet’s total for China. We could not confirm the Brazil sugar and aircraft duties because no other account we read mentions them.

Dig deeper

The BBC notes a second legal track. Over half of Canada’s tariffs rest on Section 338 of the 1930 Smoot–Hawley act, and 25 states are suing to strike down the separate forced-labour tariffs (BBC). For many trading partners, the BBC says, the duties now rest on two legal bases.

Two Italian technicians are killed in a drone strike in Kuwait, the first European civilians to die in the Iran war; Kuwait and Italy blame Iran, Tehran calls it a set-up, and Trump says the war ends “whenever I want”

Geopolitics International with US channel Continuing Independent corroboration

Continuing lead (the war ran as a lead on Nov. 22 and 24). Trigger: new verifiable fact. The first European civilian deaths of the war, an unusual Iranian denial, and Israel saying it is ready to strike Iran without U.S. forces.

Independent accounts compared: 3 newsrooms (Al Jazeera, CBS News, CNBC), plus AAA’s Florida fuel-price data.

Drones hit military sites in Kuwait on Thursday. Italy’s foreign minister, Antonio Tajani, told the Senate that one was an air-defence station where two Rheinmetall Italia technicians were servicing radar antennas; they were killed by falling shrapnel after the drones were shot down (Al Jazeera). Kuwait’s army blamed Iranian attacks. Iran’s military said it carried out no operations in the area, and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called the strike “a precooked recipe for war” (CNBC). Iran’s embassy in Rome “categorically” rejected the charge (CBS News).

Israel’s military spokesman said it is ready to strike Iran “at any moment,” even without U.S. forces, after Jared Kushner said Israel needs to leave its “wartime mindset” (CNBC). Trump told PBS late Thursday that victory was “at hand” and would come “whenever I want it to be”; on a Thanksgiving call with troops he said Iran still had “every chance to make a deal” (CNBC). At Mar-a-Lago he played down Russian and Chinese intelligence help to Tehran: “we spy on them too” (CBS).

Why it matters to you. The pump price has been falling, not rising. Florida regular averages $3.36 today, down from $3.49 a week ago and $4.05 a month ago, and Tampa–St. Petersburg is $3.33. That is still above $3.05 a year ago (AAA). A strike by Israel on its own, or a widening war that now draws in NATO allies’ citizens, is the risk to that drop. Democrats who take Congress in January want to end the war, but analysts quoted by Al Jazeera doubt their approach to Iran would differ fundamentally (Al Jazeera).

Divergence

Dispute type: attribution. Who launched the drones?

Each side’s vantage: Kuwait’s army and Italy’s foreign minister were closest to the scene and say the drones were “of Iranian origin.” Both are U.S. partners. Iran has mostly not denied its strikes on U.S. assets in Gulf states during this war. Here it denies the strike outright and offers to help investigate, which is unusual. Al Jazeera, writing from Doha, sets out both readings. No independent investigator has reported.

What it means for you: if Iran is shown to have done it, expect European pressure for tougher action; Ursula von der Leyen already called for more pressure on Iran (CBS). If not, the question becomes who wants a wider war. Either way the attribution decides whether Europe moves closer to Washington’s side.

The BBC finds Trump’s accounts bought or sold Nvidia shares at least 44 times this year, as Nvidia freezes orders from four Southeast Asian countries and chip sellers are told to rule out Chinese renters

Core — AI & tech industry Broader domestic institutions Continuing Independent corroboration

Continuing lead (Huang’s reinstatement led on Nov. 24). Trigger: new verifiable fact. The BBC’s review of Trump’s ethics disclosures, and Nvidia’s order freeze in Southeast Asia.

Independent accounts compared: 4 newsrooms (BBC, CNBC, Semafor, Breitbart), plus headlines from The Hill and the Washington Examiner that we could not read past.

BBC Verify reviewed Trump’s filings with the Office of Government Ethics and counted at least 44 Nvidia trades from January to September. Purchases were worth between $5.2 million and $17 million and sales between $4.4 million and $12 million; the filings give only ranges. His portfolios held $12.5 million to $58 million of Nvidia at the end of 2025 (BBC). The disclosures stop before Huang’s Nov. 12 resignation and his return, which Trump says he arranged. The White House says the portfolio is run by third parties and that Trump has no say in trades.

Meanwhile the export clampdown is biting. Semafor reports that Nvidia has frozen orders for buyers in Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, countries where Chinese firms have rented Nvidia chips legally; Nvidia’s Singapore arm booked $104 billion in 2025 (Semafor). Chip sellers are telling cloud renters they cannot buy Nvidia chips if any capacity will be rented to Chinese companies, under a Commerce rule in force since Nov. 19, CNBC’s sources say (CNBC). Breitbart, citing CNBC, reports that deals for thousands of B300 chips are circulating as sellers dump inventory (Breitbart). We could not confirm Semafor’s report of the order freeze. Nvidia has not commented in anything we read, and no second outlet reports it independently.

Why it matters to you. Nvidia is more than 6% of the S&P 500, by State Street’s figure cited by the BBC, so it is in most retirement accounts. The president both trades the stock and brokered its chief executive’s return, and senators are vowing to crack down on AI-chip leaks to China, with Commerce Secretary Lutnick “in the hot seat,” according to The Hill’s headline (The Hill). On the right, a Washington Examiner opinion piece is headlined “Trump shouldn’t be picking Nvidia’s CEO” (Washington Examiner).

Divergence

Dispute type: conflict of interest. Does the trading record matter?

Each side’s vantage: the White House, speaking for the president, says independent managers make every decision. The BBC worked from public filings and says it cannot tell how big the trades were, or whether any fell during this month’s swings. Democrats running for president in 2028 call the Nvidia affair corruption, according to Politico’s headline; they gain from the charge. Conservative critics object to a president choosing a company’s chief executive at all, not to the trades.

What it means for you: the November trades will appear only in Trump’s next disclosure. Until then, the documented facts are the 44 trades and his own claim that he “made the deal.”

S&P puts Nvidia’s AA rating on watch for a cut within 90 days, and Citi and JPMorgan market portfolios of AI loans to shift risk, Semafor reports

AI and financial services Core — US financials New Independent corroboration

Independent accounts compared: 3 newsrooms (CNBC, Semafor, Reuters as carried by Investing.com), plus Benzinga’s reports, read through a search index, as was the Investing.com page.

S&P Global Ratings placed Nvidia’s AA rating on CreditWatch with negative implications on Wednesday. It said a cut of one notch, to AA-, could come within 90 days if “deficiencies” in management and governance persist. It cited the long tenure of directors who have sat on the board since 1993 and the lack of a succession plan (CNBC). Moody’s, which cut its outlook to stable on Nov. 18, said Huang’s return “reduces near-term uncertainty” and left its Aa1 rating unchanged (Reuters via Investing.com). Semafor explains why the rating matters: Nvidia often backs the debt, leases and hardware purchases of weaker AI companies, which in effect lends them its credit standing (Semafor).

Separately, Semafor reports that banks including Citi and JPMorgan are marketing portfolios of AI-related loans. Some use synthetic risk transfers, which keep loans on the books while investors take the loss risk. Some banks are cutting exposure; others want room to lend more (Semafor). Benzinga, relaying the report, says Citi declined to comment and that Oracle and CoreWeave default-swap spreads hit records this week, citing Reuters (Benzinga). We could not confirm the bank portfolio sales independently. Every other account we found repeats Semafor’s report, and no sizes, borrowers or buyers have been named.

Why it matters to you. This is the AI-credit thread you asked us on Nov. 17 to weight up, and it touches JPMorgan directly. When banks pass AI risk to funds and insurers, the risk moves out of sight rather than going away, as Semafor puts it. That matters for anyone holding bank stocks or credit funds, and it makes AI borrowing cost more if Nvidia’s rating falls.

Divergence

Dispute type: interpretation. Did Huang’s return fix the governance problem?

Each agency’s vantage: S&P, writing Wednesday after the reinstatement, says the return “does not sufficiently answer” questions about board oversight. It plans to meet the board and would take a harder view if export rules were “circumvented or violated,” Benzinga reports (Benzinga). Moody’s, the same day, calls the return stabilising and the new compliance committee “constructive.” Both see the same public facts and weigh the board differently.

What it means for you: a split between rating agencies usually ends when one of them acts. The S&P meeting with Nvidia’s board, and anything that comes out of the export investigations, will decide it.

Dig deeper

On the day of Moody’s Nov. 18 move, Bloomberg reported Nvidia’s five-year default swaps at 121 basis points, about 20 lower than a week earlier. Credit markets were calmer than the stock (Bloomberg via Moneycontrol). In May the FT reported that JPMorgan and MUFG had spent more than six months selling $38 billion of construction debt for an Oracle-leased data-center project (FT). The strain on bank balance sheets was visible six months ago.

A second federal judge blocks Trump’s new birthright-citizenship order for a certified class of children

Core — US politics & policyPrimary source

Independent accounts compared: 1 newsroom (Reason’s Volokh Conspiracy), plus the court record on GovInfo and Justia and Fox News’s headline.

Judge Christopher Cooper in Washington granted a class-wide preliminary injunction on Nov. 25 against Executive Order 14418. The order tried to deny citizenship to U.S.-born children whose parents work for foreign governments without diplomatic immunity, children born through commercial surrogacy, and children with a parent suspected of gang or terror links. Cooper held that after the Supreme Court’s ruling in Trump v. Barbara the president “cannot carve out new exceptions” (court record, GovInfo; opinion, Justia). The class covers children born on or after Aug. 6. Reason’s Volokh Conspiracy calls it very similar to a September Maryland ruling, with a slightly wider class (Reason). Fox’s headline stresses that Trump had called the judge a “hater” (Fox News). A joint status report is due Dec. 4.
